Workshop and Shop Clearances

So that a watchmaker's life's work lives on

Behind every watchmaking workshop lie decades of craftsmanship, patience and experience. When a business closes – due to retirement, after a death or because no successor can be found – this step is often difficult. We support you with calm, respect and understanding.

We clear watchmaking workshops and watch and clock shops throughout Germany. Machines, tools, spare parts, watches, clocks and specialist literature: nothing should carelessly end up on the scrap heap. Instead, we carefully restore the pieces and pass them on to the next generation of watchmakers. This way, the tools stay in good hands – and the craft stays alive.

What we take care of for you

  • On-site inspection
  • Purchase of complete workshops, inventory and spare parts
  • Dismantling and removal of machines and inventory
  • Broom-clean handover of the premises on request
  • Discreet and straightforward handling

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it worth servicing an old watch?
Often, yes. Many watches and clocks that have been lying in a drawer for years run reliably again after servicing. A well-maintained, working timepiece is also worth more than one that has stopped. We will tell you honestly whether it is worth the effort.

How often should a watch or clock be serviced?
Wristwatches roughly every 5 to 7 years, large clocks every 10 to 15 years at the latest.

Do you also repair watches and clocks that other workshops have turned down?
Yes, that is exactly what we are set up for. Old timepieces for which spare parts are no longer available are our speciality.

Do you collect large clocks from my home?
Yes. We collect your large clock from you, bring it back after the work is done and set it up again.

Can I send my watch by post?

Do you also repair quartz watches?
Yes, we also repair quartz watches on request.

How can I pay?
